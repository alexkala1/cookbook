# Heirloom: everyday usefulness review

Consultation date: 2026-09-29. Provider: TypeSafe, model jev-1.13.0.

## Basis and limits

The owner wants the app to make everyday life easier. The consultation used current source capabilities: recipe collections, pantry matching, staples and quantity updates, market shopping, cooking guidance, timers, substitutions, rescue advice, recipe import, family variations and backups. Source inspection confirms capability, not usability. No user interviews, observed dinner sessions, retention data or measured time savings were available.

Jev returns choices, not explanations. These are selections among supplied alternatives, conditional on our framing; confidence is not a probability of commercial success. The experiment and thresholds below are our proposed operational plan, not prose generated by Jev.

## Smallest next experiment

Recruit five willing busy home cooks, including the owner, for an actual weekday dinner. Recruitment and sessions have not happened; nobody has been contacted.

1. Ask what they would normally do for dinner and record time available, household servings and constraints.
2. Ask them to use Heirloom to find a feasible dinner using a few ingredients they already have. Do not require full inventory setup. Record any required quantities, unavailable recipes, mismatched names/units and facilitator assistance.
3. Let them choose, resolve missing ingredients and cook. Observe discovery of existing substitutions, shopping and cooking controls without coaching unless they get stuck.
4. Record whether dinner was completed, where they left the app, extra work caused, and a 1–5 answer to “Did this make dinner easier than your usual method?” Include a short explanation.
5. After seven days, ask whether they voluntarily used it for another dinner and why or why not. Keep session notes with participant consent; automatic analytics are unnecessary for this pilot.

Record: participant code, time to feasible meal choice, setup time, manual fields entered, assistance, blockers, completed dinner, ease rating, repeat use and explanation. Compare with their normal approach; do not claim measured savings from self-report alone.

## Proposed pilot gates

These are provisional learning targets, not validated benchmarks:

- At least 4/5 select a feasible dinner within five minutes without full pantry setup or facilitator rescue.
- At least 4/5 complete their chosen meal and rate the experience easier than their usual approach.
- At least 3/5 voluntarily use it for another dinner within seven days.

Five sessions can reveal friction; they cannot establish population-wide demand or market success. Inspect individual failures even when targets pass.

## Smallest improvement to test after observing

If ingredient entry dominates, prototype a temporary “ingredients I have tonight” input that does not require maintaining a complete inventory. Clearly distinguish ingredient presence from sufficient quantity; never label an unverified dish fully in stock.

If decision overload dominates, prototype three feasible dinner choices constrained by available time and servings, with missing ingredients plainly visible. Reuse existing recipe data and cooking flow.

If neither dominates, fix the observed blocker instead. Do not implement both hypotheses by default. Preserve core reliability, allergy safeguards and usable controls while deferring unrelated expansion.

Success means people finish dinner with less effort and choose to return. It is not the number of shipped features, stored recipes or page views.
